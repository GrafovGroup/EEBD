## 6. Equipment taxonomy and technical integrity

Keep these equipment classes explicit in data, navigation, comparison content, quotations, and instructions:

1. Supplied-air or oxygen-based emergency escape devices for their specified escape applications.
2. Filtering escape hoods or respirators, if later added, as a distinct category with their own limitations and approval conditions.
3. Working, entry, rescue, or firefighting self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA), outside the initial catalogue unless separately approved.
4. Non-operational training devices and manufacturer-approved training configurations.
5. Compatible storage, carrying, mounting, and training accessories.

Some escape devices are technically self-contained apparatus. An “EEBD versus SCBA” article must explain the intended-use distinction between escape-only and working/entry equipment, rather than claiming that the technical classes never overlap.

An EEBD does not authorize firefighting, rescue entry, entry into a hazardous atmosphere, or continued work in it. Filtering devices do not supply oxygen. Do not suggest an inexpensive filtering hood is an interchangeable substitute for an approved supplied-air or oxygen device.

Selection must account for the actual hazards, intended use, escape conditions, relevant approvals, and competent assessment. The website can collect specifications and explain product categories; it must not automatically prescribe life-safety equipment from a short quiz.

Never apply a single duration, inspection interval, refill schedule, service life, or approval statement across the whole range. Store rated duration with its test/rating standard and conditions. Distinguish a product type-approval certificate's validity from an individual unit's manufacture date, service-life date, or service due date.

Use sources S1–S5 in Section 25 as the starting technical evidence, then verify the exact products at execution time.
